On October 19, 2026, the SGX3 program officially launched its Fall Training 2026 virtual cohort on [hackhpc.org](https://hackhpc.org/sgx3-fall-training-2026/), bringing together an energetic group of undergraduate trainees from Elizabeth City State University (ECSU), Norfolk State University (NSU), and partner institutions. Meeting virtually every Monday and Wednesday from 6:00 to 8:00 PM Eastern Time through December 30, 2026, this intensive hands-on program is structured to transform undergraduate students with zero prior High-Performance Computing (HPC) experience into capable, confident members of the national research computing community.

**Program Tagline:** "Learn. Build. Connect. Grow."

The inaugural session was hosted by an expert instructional and leadership team dedicated to student mentorship and workforce development:

- **Dr. Linda Hayden:** SGX3 Co-Principal Investigator (Co-PI) and Workforce Development Lead, bringing over 13 years of executive leadership in science gateway outreach and HBCU cyberinfrastructure capacity building.
- **Jeaime Powell:** Lead HPC Specialist, Workforce Development Instructor, and double ECSU alumnus (B.S. in Computer Science, M.S. in Applied Mathematics) with extensive expertise in supercomputing architectures and research workflows.
- **Kimora Mason:** Senior Student Assistant and graduating senior at ECSU majoring in Computer Science and Mathematics, serving as a peer mentor following years of active participation in SGX3 initiatives.

The core mission of the program is clear: equip undergraduate participants with practical command-line and cloud skills, guide them in developing a distinct professional identity through hosted portfolios, and connect them directly to national research cyberinfrastructure and internship opportunities.

## Core Curriculum Pillars & Program Logistics

To ensure students gain competitive competencies for academic research and industry careers, the SGX3 curriculum integrates rigorous technical coursework with continuous professional identity development.

### Technical and Professional Skill Pillars

- **Linux & Shell Fundamentals (Bandit System)**
  - *Career Benefit:* Trainees master command-line navigation, file system permissions, and remote terminal security using the hands-on Bandit game environment—establishing the essential operational foundation required to execute jobs on national supercomputers.
- **Python Programming & Machine Learning (Kaggle Certifications)**
  - *Career Benefit:* Completing industry-recognized Kaggle certifications in Python and Introduction to Machine Learning equips students with automated data processing capabilities and basic algorithmic logic necessary for modern computational research.
- **Version Control & Portfolio Hosting (Git & GitHub Pages)**
  - *Career Benefit:* Trainees construct a public portfolio website hosted via GitHub Pages, showcasing their professional headshot, resume, personal statement, and social links (LinkedIn, GitHub, ORCiD) to present a verified digital identity to future employers and graduate admissions committees.
- **Cloud Computing Environments (Google Cloud Fundamentals)**
  - *Career Benefit:* Trainees gain direct experience with cloud infrastructure, scalable virtualization, and remote data storage management across enterprise platforms.
- **Professional Identity & Career Readiness**
  - *Career Benefit:* Through resume refining, LinkedIn profile optimization (linked directly to the SGCI/SGX3 organizational network), technical reporting, and required applications for national programs (such as PEARC27, SC27, and USRSE), students convert technical coursework into active career advancement.

### Administrative Logistics & Stipend Requirements

Participation in the SGX3 program includes a financial stipend alongside instructional support. To deliver program benefits seamlessly, SGX3 maintains a structured administrative framework.

## Science Gateways & National Cyberinfrastructure Landscape

During Session 1, Dr. Linda Hayden provided a comprehensive overview detailing how local student projects integrate into the broader national research cyberinfrastructure ecosystem.

### Demystifying Science Gateways & SGX3 Center of Excellence

A Science Gateway is a full-stack, web-based platform that streamlines access to complex, low-level computing environments, advanced laboratory instruments, massive data repositories, and AI processing pipelines. By placing intuitive user interfaces on top of high-performance backend hardware, gateways enable researchers, educators, and students to focus on scientific discovery rather than administrative infrastructure setup.

The SGX3 Center of Excellence (funded under NSF Award 2231406, extending work from Award ACI-1547611) serves as a national hub to extend, expand, and exemplify gateway services. Under Dr. Hayden's workforce development leadership, SGX3 actively connects undergraduate institutions—particularly Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs) and Tribal Colleges—to national research opportunities.

### Key Benefits of Science Gateways

1. **Broadened Access:** Eliminates technical barriers so non-HPC experts can execute complex computations directly from a standard web browser.
2. **Accelerated Discovery:** Integrates domain-specific software tools directly with computational resources to speed up research in healthcare, renewable energy, and environmental science.
3. **Maximized Federal Investments:** Increases the utilization, efficiency, and community impact of federally funded supercomputing centers.
4. **Workforce Empowerment:** Trains the next generation of Research Software Engineers (RSEs), data scientists, and scientific leaders through structured student programs and faculty course-infusion hackathons.

### National Infrastructure & NAIRR Pilot Portal

Trainees in the SGX3 program gain exposure to national high-performance computing platforms, including ACCESS-CI, the National Research Platform (NRP), CloudBank, and the National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource (NAIRR).

A major highlight of Dr. Hayden's presentation was the institutional progression of the NAIRR Pilot Portal ([nairrpilot.org](https://nairrpilot.org)), established through SGX3 leadership across a three-phase NSF supplement evolution:

1. **First Supplement:** Standing up the initial NAIRR Pilot Portal (nairrpilot.org) to aggregate federal AI computational resources into a unified access point.
2. **Second Supplement:** Conducting a nationwide community needs survey across academic and industrial sectors to map out a 5-year strategic vision for federal AI infrastructure.
3. **Third Supplement:** Implementing survey recommendations, culminating in the formal establishment of the permanent NAIRR Operations Center awarded to the San Diego Supercomputer Center (SDSC) in partnership with the Texas Advanced Computing Center (TACC).

Today, the portal supports over 400 research projects across 44 states, connecting academic institutions directly to cutting-edge federal AI and supercomputing resources.

Understanding these national infrastructure systems paved the way for open student discussions on technology, environmental impacts, and career expectations.

### Key Discussion Recaps

#### 1. AI vs. Superintelligence (SI): Federal Terminology Shift

> **Context:** On September 29, 2026, federal science agencies—including the National Science Foundation ([nsf.gov](https://www.nsf.gov))—began executing an agency-wide terminology update, systematically transitioning official references from "Artificial Intelligence" (AI) to "Superintelligence" (SI).

- **Trainee Perspectives:**
  - **Abraham Ashade** argued that the shift reflects real-world technological leaps, noting that modern platforms (such as Claude reverse-engineering complex software architectures in minutes) represent a 10x leap over tools from three years ago, justifying an elevated classification.
  - **Bryce Cliette** and **Jayden Queen** offered a counter-perspective, emphasizing that current Large Language Models (LLMs) remain in early developmental stages. They noted that these models rely strictly on human-defined parameters and remain prone to hallucinations without true autonomous reasoning.
  - **Akira Evanson** highlighted that current models function as statistical engines executing probability calculations rather than self-aware superintelligences, cautioning against premature labeling.

#### 2. Environmental Impact of Data Centers & Cooling System Engineering

> **Context:** Trainees raised critical questions regarding the localized environmental impacts of massive research data centers, focusing on energy consumption, localized heat discharge, and water depletion.

- **Localized Community Impacts:** Dr. Linda Hayden shared that several Tribal College communities have voiced strong opposition to rapid data center expansion on sovereign lands due to natural resource depletion. Kimora Mason cited local reports from a California community where ambient neighborhood temperatures rose by 16 degrees Fahrenheit due to heat discharge from a nearby data center facility.
- **Cooling Systems Architecture Compared:** Jeaime Powell provided a technical comparison between data center cooling designs:
  - **Open-Loop Cooling Systems:** These systems siphon groundwater directly from wells, run it through heat exchangers, and discharge the heated wastewater into the environment. Powell noted that approximately 70% of global data centers and 45% of U.S. data centers still rely on open-loop designs. The raw groundwater contains heavy mineral and metal contaminants that cause severe pipe corrosion within 5 to 10 years while severely depleting local water tables.
  - **Closed-Loop Recirculating Systems:** Modern facilities—such as the Texas Advanced Computing Center (TACC)—utilize large, sealed, treated water reservoirs (e.g., TACC's 2-million-gallon recirculating loop). Treated water continually recirculates through closed exchangers, eliminating groundwater contamination, preserving local aquifers, and extending hardware lifespan.

#### 3. Practical AI Tools & MetaMuse Integration

> **Context:** Powell demonstrated how modern AI platforms transition from passive search engines into active daily workflow assistants and custom software generators.

- **Jeaime Powell's Live Demonstrations:** Powell shared personal workflows to illustrate how AI reclaims developer time. Rather than searching for pre-existing software that partially meets a need, Powell described using Claude to generate a custom software development plan, passing that plan to specialized coding models, and deploying custom applications in minutes. Real-world examples demonstrated during the session included:
  - **Hardware & App Automation:** Building a custom microcontroller hardware rig paired with a Claude-coded application in 30 minutes to automate tedious, repetitive mobile gaming tasks (gift delivery, mega evolutions, level grinding in Pokémon Go).
  - **Digital Pet Creation:** Developing a custom 1980s Moon Patrol-themed digital pet app from scratch in half an hour.
  - **Workflow Delegation ("Anna"):** Deploying MetaMuse (muse.ai) assistant "Anna" to draft technical documentation, manage automated task responses in Slack, and resolve routine student administrative queries.
- **MetaMuse Incentive Structure:** Powell introduced MetaMuse (muse.ai), highlighting its dual-referral model where both the inviter and the invitee receive 1 billion tokens each to test workflow automation and multi-agent collaboration.
