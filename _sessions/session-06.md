## Unlocking National Supercomputing Ecosystems with ACCESS-CI

On November 4, 2026, the SGX3 Fall Training cohort met for Session 6, focusing on national cyberinfrastructure onboarding and intermediate Linux command-line mastery. Lead instructor Je'aime Powell opened the session by reviewing the cohort's progress across task sheet milestones, including Slack setup, Kaggle Python certification, Vim essentials, Git fundamentals, and LinkedIn optimization.

The session centered on **Task Sheet Item #12**, which requires scholars to register for an account on a national High-Performance Computing (HPC), Artificial Intelligence (AI), or Super Intelligence (SI) platform. Powell walked students through the registration portal for **ACCESS-CI** ([access-ci.org](https://access-ci.org/)), the primary gateway to federally funded research computing, and emphasized using university `.edu` email addresses to streamline institutional verification. Powell explained that an active ACCESS-CI account grants students single-sign-on access to a broad ecosystem of advanced computing environments. These include **CloudBank** for provisioning free cloud virtual machines, the **National Research Platform (NRP)** for running containerized workloads, and national supercomputing clusters.

## The Evolution of NAIRR and AI/SI Reflection Assignments

During the portal walkthrough, Powell highlighted a major recent update regarding the **NAIRR Pilot Portal** ([nairrpilot.org](https://nairrpilot.org/)). Federal agencies transitioned the naming convention of NAIRR to stand for the **National Architecture for Innovation, Research, and Resource-sharing**, aligning with the federal shift toward Super Intelligence (SI) terminology.

To complete Task Sheet Item #12, Powell assigned a brief reflection task. Trainees were instructed to use AI/SI tools to evaluate how national HPC and cloud resources could enhance their academic coursework or scientific research. For example, students can explain how ACCESS-CI supercomputers enable large-scale class simulations or how NAIRR datasets support undergraduate machine learning experiments.

## Hands-On Bandit Mechanics and Peer-Led Walkthroughs

Following the cyberinfrastructure orientation, student mentor **Professor Lo (Dillon Moore)** took the lead to guide the cohort through **OverTheWire Bandit Levels 5 through 8**, supported by **Suniyah "Suni" Esey** in the chat.

The hands-on technical demonstration reinforced core Linux command-line mechanics:

- **Directory Navigation and Inspection:** Trainees practiced changing working directories with `cd`, listing directory contents using `ls` and `ls -la`, and inspecting file contents with `cat`.
- **Searching Complex Directories:** Students learned how to navigate nested file trees (such as the `inhere` directory) and filter files based on specific properties, such as human-readable ASCII formatting, file size, and non-executable permissions.
- **Terminal Control and Troubleshooting:** Suni and student Bryson Clark reminded participants to use `Ctrl+C` to terminate hung commands and return to the prompt. Powell also opened Zoom breakout rooms to provide targeted one-on-one terminal assistance for students resolving connection timeouts and SSH configurations.
